My Students’ Pages

Portfolios and reflections published by the graduate students I have supervised.

Supervising a master’s project is the part of academic work I find most rewarding: a student arrives with a question about practice, and leaves with evidence. Several of these projects went on to peer-reviewed publication, and the students have built their own pages describing the work in their own words.

Master’s · April 2021

Dalia M. Alzyod

Dalia Alzyod’s master’s research asked how age and gender shape tongue strength and tongue endurance in children in the United Arab Emirates, comparing typically developing children with children who have idiopathic speech sound disorders. She measured both using the Iowa Oral Performance Instrument (IOPI). The study was published in PLOS ONE in 2023, and she presented it at the Emirates Speech-Language Pathology & Audiology Congress in Dubai in 2024. She is also a co-author on a forthcoming paper examining how reliably tongue pressure and endurance can be measured in Emirati children from one week to the next.

Master’s · December 2021

Mariam Al Shamsi

Mariam Al Shamsi’s master’s research examined how teachers, specialists, and parents judge the effectiveness of the Picture Exchange Communication System (PECS) for children with autism in classrooms in the United Arab Emirates — bringing three different vantage points to bear on the same intervention. The work was published in BMC Psychology in 2022 and contributed to a wider program of research on culturally responsive communication support for children with autism in the region.

Master’s · December 2021

Alia Albedwawi

Alia Albedwawi’s master’s research investigated how special education teachers perceive the use of educational games for teaching mathematical concepts to children with Down syndrome — whether play-based instruction genuinely helps children grasp ideas that are otherwise difficult to teach. The study is in press with Frontiers in Education.

Master’s · April 2021

Aysha Al Kaabi

Aysha Al Kaabi’s master’s research developed language stimulation activities for children from birth to 36 months of age — practical material for the earliest period of communication development, when the people around a child do most of the work of building language.
